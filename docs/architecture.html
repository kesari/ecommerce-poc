Gortex, Graphify, SCIP, and agents

They solve different parts of the same problem. That distinction matters more than their feature lists.

SCIP tells you what is statically true.

The compiler knows that two references point to the same Java symbol. This is the strongest evidence for code-level relationships.

Gortex tells you what may be affected.

It joins service-level relationships such as HTTP consumers, endpoint providers, topic publishers, and topic subscribers.

Graphify gives an agent a graph to explore.

It creates broad structural context from code, documents, configuration, and concepts, with a relatively simple setup.

Claude and Codex investigate.

They search, connect evidence, understand business meaning, and explain the result—but their completeness depends on where they searched.

The architecture we are testing

SCIP+Gortex+Git, docs, tests→Claude / Codex→Evidence-backed change report

The agent is the reasoner, not the source of truth. Exact code evidence, service topology, and semantic reasoning stay distinguishable in the final answer.

What would justify Gortex?

It must find critical REST, Kafka, schema, or cross-language consumers that SCIP plus intelligent agent search misses. If it cannot do that reliably, the extra graph layer is difficult to justify.
